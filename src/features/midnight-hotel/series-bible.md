# Midnight Hotel: The Guest Who Wasn't on the List

Original ten-story illustrated serial for approximately ages 8–12, especially Grades 4–5. Second-person present tense: the reader is “you,” never given an appearance/name/gender. Nia and Bark Bolt are co-leads. Read for pleasure only: no exercises, quizzes, prompts, required responses, scores, locks, or reading streaks.

## Evidence translated into editorial decisions

The research brief is maintained in the project documentation. Child surveys support humor, worlds to explore, visual appeal, and choice; author testimony supports pace, character voices, controlled suspense, and planning clues. These are editorial applications, not a proven formula. The reader's expressed preference for this hotel is the strongest specific signal.

Deliver an immediate strange situation, specific character wants, a plan that meets a real complication, and an earned resolution. Each complete story: 800–1,050 words across four titled sections, paragraphs generally 1–4 sentences, substantial dialogue, clear Grade 4–5 prose without baby talk. No moral-summary endings. A few enjoyable quiet moments matter. End each episode with a resolved local problem and a brief new question. Story 10 resolves the season; don't substitute another cliffhanger for the answer.

## World and character invariants

The hotel appears at sunset between a dentist and a supermarket; monster guests eat “breakfast” at sunset because their day begins then. It has twelve numbered guest-room doors in ordinary use, a lobby, dining room, kitchen, service corridor, lift, laundry, conservatory, ballroom and roof. All ten adventures occur over several successive evenings; school/home need no detailed subplot. You can leave safely at dawn; nobody is trapped or threatened with death.

- Nia: ten, brown skin, dark tightly curled hair in two puffs, mustard-yellow raincoat over teal overalls, red high-top trainers. Observant, inventive, quick with a dry remark. Sometimes prizes a neat explanation too much; can admit a mistake. Wants to be taken seriously. Does not deliver a lesson speech.
- Bark Bolt: scruffy small-to-medium terrier, honey-tan and cream fur, one floppy ear, teal security vest and oversized brass badge. Inflated official language, food investigations, terrified of the vacuum. Brave when a friend needs him; never only a fool. Recurring “evidence snacks” joke changes, doesn't repeat verbatim ten times.
- Madam Morrow: kindly, brisk, broad-shouldered older witch hotel manager; aubergine coat, swept silver hair, half-moon spectacles. Competent and caring, busy repairing the hotel; kids take initiative without adults becoming useless. Does not know who changes the labels. When told the truth, she acts.
- Countess Vesper: tall elegant plum-and-black vampire, burgundy bob, tiny nonthreatening fangs, magnificent pink rabbit slippers. Pride is funny; appearance is not mocked. Secretly shy, likes gardening and ballroom dance. Innocent of the label changes.
- Chef Mist: round pearly-white ghost with curling wisp tail, tall chef hat, copper whisk. Expressive and gentle, terrible at keeping food inside his own body.
- Mr. Sizzle: chunky friendly turquoise dragon with apricot belly and little brass spectacles. Big appetite and a beautiful singing voice, sensitive about embarrassing accidents. No dangerous fire aimed at people.
- Mallow: small lilac ghost with soft rounded shape, dark expressive eyes, burgundy bellhop cap, satchel stitched with blue thread. Shy, practical, good at repairs. First glimpsed briefly in story 2, more visible in 5/7. Must NOT be revealed as culprit before story 8. Claims to be a temporary porter. Silver ink smudges and blue thread are visible fair clues. Wants a permanent place to belong. Not an evil enemy.

## Magic rules (plant before payoff)

1. Enchanted hotel objects follow written job cards literally. People/guests never lose free will. Silver correction ink can temporarily redirect ONE object's existing job; it cannot create new powers. Removing a silver label restores that object's original job. Ordinary ink does not enchant objects.
2. Mallow has a battered blue trunk containing an unused folded spare room. It was stored after an old storm. It can become Room 13, but its instructions were separated: a reversed plan (story3), a brass key stamped13 (story4), a hinge diagram (story5), and opening instructions in an old maintenance song (story6).
3. The spare room unfolds only into the space between the twelve existing rooms when all twelve guest-room doors are open and its keyed latch is turned. This is a physical/cooperative task, not a new magical spell. This requirement is planted in3, clarified6/9, used10.
4. Mallow redirects objects with silver labels to collect the scattered room parts and delay being discovered; his fixes cause real inconvenience. His motive does not excuse this. He fears being rejected for arriving without a booking, even though Morrow would have helped. In8 he is caught and stops. In9 he confesses and helps return every label. In10 he asks for help. Guests agree to open their doors; the reader coordinates a practical twelve-door solution using objects/friends established earlier. Adults assist.

## Art direction

High-quality original illustrated animated-feature storybook aesthetic, richly painted tactile textures, clean expressive silhouettes, strong warm amber lighting against plum/teal twilight, funny tiny details, kid-friendly approachable monsters. Not generic clipart or repeated hotel facades. Nia/Bolt match every time. Story-specific scene composition. Avoid horror realism, gore, threatening eyes/teeth, brands/copyrighted characters, and illegible generated text. Each chapter has its own illustration and alt text; page UI supplies titles. Hero shows the cast in a magical hotel lobby with a grand violet staircase and warm windows, no text.
